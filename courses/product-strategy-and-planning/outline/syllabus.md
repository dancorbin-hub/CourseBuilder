## Advanced AI for Product Strategy and Planning

Syllabus and course outline. Draft for Pragmatic review, 2026-11-26.

Planning here means annual and quarterly product planning. The course maps to Capability 2, Strategic Direction.


### Course promise

After this course, you can build and manage one AI agent that assembles your portfolio pack, request decisions, business case, roadmap changes and outcome reports with checked evidence. You make every strategy, priority and investment call yourself.

### Who this course is for

- Experienced product managers who run quarterly reviews, request intake, business cases, roadmaps or outcome reviews.
- Product leaders, portfolio leads and planning leads who set priorities and defend investment.
- PMs in software, hardware, services and internal platform teams.

This course is not an introduction to AI prompts. It does not teach how to write a strategy, how to score opportunities or how to build a Product Canvas. You bring the strategy. The agent helps you check your work against it.

No Pragmatic core course is a prerequisite. The course defines each Pragmatic term when it first uses it. Some experience with building agents or skills helps.


### What you learn to do

You become the agent manager. The agent does the routine evidence work. You do the work that needs product judgment:

- Write the agent's brief: its job, inputs, quality checks, hand-off rules and output.
- Set quality checks that the agent runs on its own output.
- Set hand-off rules: when the agent stops and gives an item to you.
- Read the flag report and decide each item.
- Audit a sample of the agent's output against its sources and its numbers.
- Tune the brief for what the audit found, and run again.
- Record each decision with a reason that cites the evidence.

The big idea. Strategy is where product judgment is essential. AI can gather, total, trace and compare far more than you can read by hand. It cannot know what your company should do. The agent gathers the evidence. You bring product sense, experience and judgment, and you make the call.

Ideas that run through the day:

- There is no magic number. You set every range, tolerance and threshold in the brief. The agent reports the evidence against it.
- Be great at product management before you are great at AI.
- The roadmap is direction. The release plan is the promise. The agent never turns one into the other.

### Course objectives

By the end of the course, you can:

- Build a strategy evidence agent from a template on your platform and run it on your own planning inputs.
- Write and tune the agent brief (job, inputs, quality checks, hand-off rules, output) after each audit, with every range and threshold set by you.
- Decide each flag report item and audit a sample of agent output against its sources and its numbers.
- Connect agent jobs so that one job's output feeds the next, with hand-off rules that stop the agent before any strategy, priority, funding or release plan call.
- Record each strategy, request, business case, roadmap and investment decision yourself, with a written reason that cites the evidence.

### How the course works

One agent, five jobs. You build the Strategy Evidence Agent in module 1 from a template. Each later module adds one job. You leave with one agent that does five jobs: portfolio pack, requests, business case, roadmap and outcome tracking.

The agent never sets strategy. It never ranks priorities, fills a decision field or recommends funding, a priority, an investment change or a release date. Every job has a hand-off rule that stops the agent before such a step. The decision field stays blank until you write in it.

Terms used in this course:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Problem backlog | Where a team tracks unsolved market problems and the evidence for each. It feeds the product backlog |
| Validated problem backlog | The problems you have validated by your own rule, with their evidence |
| Market | All buyers who have the problem the product solves |
| Market segment | A group inside the market with shared needs that the team can reach |
| Evaluator adoption | The share of the segment expected to evaluate a solution of this kind |
| Projected share | The share of the segment the team expects to win. It comes only from your own internal data |
| Roadmap | Direction: outcome themes and timeframes. Not a promise |
| Release plan | The promise: dated commitments that the delivery team owns. The agent never edits it |
| Outcome and output | An outcome is a change in customer or business results. An output is work the team produced or shipped |

The agent climbs a ladder. Each module gives the agent more room, and you check it in a different way.

| Step | What the agent does | What you check | Modules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hand-run batch | Runs on a batch you give it | Every flag and a sample | 1 |
| 1 plus. Hand-run batch with a decision checkpoint | Stops, leaves the decision field blank and waits for you | Each decision card before the agent continues | 2 |
| 2. Refresh with a "what changed" report | Updates a living record when new evidence arrives | The "what changed" note and the flag report | 3 |
| 3. Hand-off between jobs | One job's output feeds the next job | The hand-off rule and traced items | 4 |
| 3 plus. Monthly run across jobs | Runs each month on new exports and reads other jobs' records | Only the flag report and a sample | 5 |

Each lab has a planted AI error. In module 1 we tell you that it is there. In modules 2 to 4 you get a hint. In module 5 you get no hint. The debrief shows where it was and which check or audit found it.

Each lab has a check sample. After you fix the brief, you run the agent on a second sample and compare the result with a pass condition. In modules 1, 2 and 5 you run it in the debrief. In modules 3 and 4 the instructor shows the result, and you run it after class in about 5 minutes.

Your own work first. Use your own plans, requests, business case, roadmap and metrics when your company allows it. If you cannot, use the case company: Larkspan Systems, a fictional maker of wireless temperature sensors and the cloud software that watches them. It has product teams for external customers and an internal platform team. It has a strategy, OKRs and KPIs. One case serves the whole course.

You do not fall behind. Each module has a catch-up kit. You can rebuild the agent at the start of any module in about 5 minutes.


### Modules

Minutes show the one-day format first, then the two-half-day format where it differs.


#### Module 1. The quarterly portfolio pack: is the work consistent with the strategy? (70 minutes; 80 in half days)

Promise: Hand the quarterly portfolio pack to an agent that totals, links and flags, and manage it by its checks and flags.

- Workflow: Assemble the quarterly portfolio review pack and check that planned work stays consistent with the stated strategy.
- Bottleneck it removes: One person stitches team updates by hand, so the pack is stale before the review. The meeting becomes data reconciliation instead of decisions. Few teams can show that planned work links to the stated strategy.
- Objectives:
- Build the Strategy Evidence Agent from the template, with your strategy overview, OKRs and KPIs in its brief.
- Decide each flag report item, including work with no clear link to a stated objective.
- Audit capacity totals for 2 shared teams and 3 linked items, and record what you find.
- Add a quality check for what the audit found, rerun, and make fund, pause or stop calls with a reason.
- Lab: Build the agent. Run a quarter of team submissions. Read the strategy consistency section and the flag report. Audit, tune and rerun on the next quarter. A fast-start card per platform helps learners who are new to the builders.
- Your input: Your team plan submissions, a status export and capacity by team for one quarter, or the case submissions.
- Learner evidence: Your tuned brief (with one new check), your flag report decisions, your audit findings for 2 shared teams and 3 linked items, and your fund, pause or stop calls with reasons.
- Stretch: Compare 2 funding scenarios that you name, and show what slips. You write both scenarios. The agent does not propose one. Turn the job into a custom skill.

#### Module 2. Requests: the agent builds the case, you make the call (60 minutes; 75 in half days)

Promise: Answer every request with a reason, not silence, and keep every decision yours.

- Workflow: Decide on each week's feature and stakeholder requests against the problem backlog, the stated strategy and past decisions, and close the loop with each requester.
- Bottleneck it removes: Requests arrive through many channels, and every requester believes theirs is the most urgent. The loudest voice wins, decisions get reopened, and requesters hear silence instead of a reason.
- Objectives:
- Extend the agent with a request job that builds one decision card per request and leaves the decision field blank.
- Audit 3 cards and 2 cited decision log entries against the source requests.
- Add a matching rule to the brief for what the audit found.
- Write yes, no or later and a reason on each flagged card, then check that each reply matches your decision.
- Lab: Add the request job. Run a week of requests. Read the decision cards and the flag report. Audit, tune, write your decisions, then let the agent draft the replies.
- Your input: One week of requests (8 to 15) and any record of past decisions, or the case requests.
- Learner evidence: Your tuned brief with the matching rule, your flag report decisions, your audit findings, your decision cards with yes, no or later and a reason, and reply drafts that match each decision.
- Stretch: Read requests from a shared intake form or tracker, on the platforms whose step card includes this step.

#### Module 3. The living business case: assumptions, red team and what changed (70 minutes; 65 in half days)

Promise: Keep the business case current, so each commit decision rests on fresh evidence.

- Workflow: Keep the business case assumption register current and red-team it before each decision point.
- Bottleneck it removes: Business cases go stale during execution, and teams rarely recheck the original assumptions. Hardware commits in stages, and a wrong decision costs more at each stage. Published market estimates differ because they measure different scopes.
- Objectives:
- Extend the agent to keep an assumption register with evidence, date, owner and effect, using a sheet that holds the formulas.
- Audit the evidence for the 2 assumptions with the largest effect and record what you find.
- Add a hand-off rule, refresh with new evidence and read the "what changed" note.
- State your gate recommendation (go, hold or stop) and the next assumption to test, with a reason.
- Lab: Add the business case job and the course sheet. Run the register and sensitivity. Read the red team note. Audit, add a rule and refresh with new evidence.
- Your input: One business case or investment proposal and its model, or the case business case.
- Learner evidence: Your tuned brief with one new hand-off rule, your flag report decisions, your audit findings for 2 assumptions, and your gate recommendation with the next assumption to test.
- Stretch: Refresh the market size inputs by live web search, one link per number, on the platforms whose step card includes this step. Build a buy, build or partner pack for one component, if time allows.

#### Module 4. Roadmap refresh and change: trace every item, guard every promise (65 minutes; 60 in half days)

Promise: Know the evidence behind each roadmap item, and see what a change displaces before you decide.

- Workflow: Refresh roadmap themes each quarter with a per-item evidence trace to the validated problem backlog. Then assess each roadmap change: what it displaces, dependency hits, a decision log entry and a standing commitment guard against the release plan.
- Bottleneck it removes: A roadmap that nobody governs becomes a historical record. Tracing each item to evidence and strategy takes manual work each cycle. Shared roadmaps become de facto commitments, so every change needs a change log and one version of the plan.
- Objectives:
- Extend the agent to trace each roadmap item to a theme, a validated problem and a stated objective, and list items with no trace.
- Audit 3 roadmap items against the evidence log, and add a rule to the brief for what you find.
- Write the hand-off rule that stops the agent on any change that touches the release plan, and read the rule that passes an approved request into the roadmap job.
- Decide each flag on a roadmap change and write the decision log entry with your reason.
- Lab: Add the roadmap job. Write the release plan hand-off rule. Run the refresh. Audit and tune. Send a change request from module 2 and read the impact note, the commitment guard and the flag report.
- Your input: Your current roadmap, validated problem backlog or equivalent, release plan and one real change request, or the case roadmap and change request.
- Learner evidence: Your release plan hand-off rule as written, your tuned brief, your flag report decisions, your audit findings for 3 items, and your decision log entry with decision and reason.
- Stretch: Build the change assessment as a second agent that reads the first agent's output, on the platforms whose step card includes this step.

#### Module 5. Outcomes, not output: the monthly check against the business case (55 minutes; 50 in half days)

Promise: Check each month whether the business case outcomes moved, not only whether work shipped.

- Workflow: Track realized outcomes against the business case and OKRs each month, and flag measures that are off track for your investment review.
- Bottleneck it removes: After approval, few teams compare results with the business case baseline. Reports rely on manual status updates, and output measures stand in for outcomes.
- Objectives:
- Extend the agent to track each measure against the business case baseline and the OKRs, labeled outcome or output.
- Write the off-target range and the number of months in the brief.
- Audit 2 "on track" measures against the business case, and write the outcome-or-output rule for what you find.
- Write an investment review note: keep, change or stop, with a reason that cites the measures.
- Lab: Add the outcome job and the course sheet. Set your off-target range. Run two months of metrics. Read only the flag report and a sample. In the debrief, fix the brief and run the check sample.
- Your input: Two months of metric exports for one product or initiative, plus its business case measures or OKRs, aggregated, or the case exports.
- Learner evidence: Your off-target range and hand-off rules, your flag report decisions, your audit findings for 2 measures, your tuned brief and your investment review note.
- Stretch: Run each month on a schedule, on the platforms whose step card includes this step.

### What you need before class

You get a platform readiness checklist before class. Run it. It takes a few minutes and finds access problems before the class starts. The checklist asks you to upload one test .xlsx file. If you use Gemini, it asks you to open a Google Sheet copy.

The core lab steps are the same on all four platforms. Some stretch steps (a scheduled run, a second agent, a live web search) are only on the step cards of platforms that support them. Copilot Studio, Power Automate and Google Antigravity are not used in this course.

| Platform | What you build | What you need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claude | A Project with instructions and knowledge files | Any plan. Code execution and file creation must be on. Your org owner can turn them off. Web search on, for the stretch steps |
| ChatGPT | A Project with instructions and files | ChatGPT Plus or higher. This is a course prerequisite. The numeric steps use ChatGPT Work file output. If you have a lower plan, you pair with a Plus learner on the day |
| Microsoft Copilot | An agent in Copilot agent builder | Permission to create agents in agent builder. A Microsoft 365 Copilot license or pay-as-you-go billing for knowledge files. Not GCC. Learners with Copilot Chat only use a reduced path that runs one job at a time |
| Gemini | A Gemini skill with instructions and files | The Gemini app on a personal or Workspace account. You use a Google Sheet copy for the numeric steps |

Also bring:

- Your company's rules on approved AI tools, connectors and sharing. The course open has a "know before you design" check.
- Your own inputs if your company allows it: team plan submissions, a week of requests, a business case, a roadmap with its release plan, and two months of metrics. If you cannot use them, the case data is ready for you.

No connectors are needed. You upload or paste files.


### Data safety

Every lab has this note:

> Use an approved tool only. Do not put client data, health data protected by HIPAA or personal data (PII) into a tool your company has not approved. Do not put unreleased roadmaps or release dates, forecasts, pricing, supplier terms or other non-public financial data into a tool your company has not approved. Do not put confidential names in web search queries.

Before each lab you answer a short data check: is the tool approved, does your file hold sensitive data, and will you use your own work or the case. An agent that keeps a pack, a request log, a register or a decision log keeps sensitive data longer than a one-off prompt. In each lab you decide what the log may keep, and for how long.
