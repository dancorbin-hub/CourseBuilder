### Notes on Part A

1. Dan's memory does not match one study. It is probably a blend of A1 (younger adults more open), A4 (36% of users say LLMs are more helpful) and A5 (1 in 4 more likely to talk to a chatbot).
2. Do not say "younger generations prefer AI therapists". The 2026 data says the reverse for most people: two-thirds are uncomfortable (A1) and people rate humans more favorably (A2).
3. Usable 2026 wording: "In 2026, 37% of US adults under 30 said they would be comfortable working with an AI therapist instead of an in-person one, about twice the rate of older adults (YouGov, May 2026)." This is "open to", not "preferred".
4. A3: the primary RAND report was not opened. Open it before use.
5. A4 and A5 fail the 2026 rule. Report only as background if Dan approves.

### Notes on Part B

1. Best 2026 source: B1 (POQ, peer reviewed, randomized, n = 2,243). It shows acceptance: satisfaction is almost unchanged and no extra drop-out. It compares AI interviews to fixed surveys, not to human interviewers.
2. On AI vs human interviewers, 2026 evidence does not show a preference for AI. B2 and B3 (probability panel) lean to humans. Most B3 respondents had no preference. Opt-in panel members (the usual market research panel) leaned to AI.
3. Accurate wording: "Respondents accept AI-moderated interviews. Satisfaction barely changes (POQ, 2026). Most have no preference between AI and human interviewers. Where they do, it depends on the panel (Surgo Health preprint, 2026)."
4. Do not use B4 for a preference claim. Its title says "prefer" but it gives no measured preference.
5. No 2026 source found on B2B buyers specifically. Record as a gap.
